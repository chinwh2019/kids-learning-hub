# Story review

All three stories are warm, short and easy for children aged about 4 to 8 to follow. Each one names its source and ends with a lesson and a question, which is the right shape for bedtime. The app keeps your text word for word. The suggestions below are optional.

## 1. The man who helped a thirsty dog
- Faithful to the hadith (for example Sahih al-Bukhari 2363, Sahih Muslim 2244). Add the numbers to the `source` line, as story 3 does. Please check them against your own copy.
- The hadith says **"Allah thanked him and forgave him."** "Allah thanked him" is a beautiful detail for children and could replace "Allah was so pleased".
- The companions then asked whether there is reward for being kind to animals, and the Prophet ﷺ answered: "There is a reward for kindness to every living being." This fits your last paragraph and could be the closing line.

## 2. Prophet Sulayman and the little ant
- Surah An-Naml 27:16 says Sulayman was taught **the speech of birds**. "The speech of animals" goes a little further than the verse. You could say: "He taught him the speech of birds, and let him hear the tiny ant too."
- **"So the great king stopped his whole army"** is not stated in the Quran. It is a fair reading, but since the story cites the Quran, you could soften it: "We can imagine the great king made sure his army stepped carefully."
- The dua is shortened. The verse continues: "and admit me, by Your mercy, among Your righteous servants." Consider adding it. The app shows the Arabic of 27:19 (the first part) under that page, so please have it checked.
- Calling the ant "her" matches the Quran, which uses the feminine form. A fun fact for kids: the surah itself is named An-Naml, "The Ant".

## 3. The honest men and the jar of gold
- Faithful to Sahih al-Bukhari 3472 and Sahih Muslim 1721.
- In the hadith the judge says "spend on yourselves from it and give in charity", so "spend some of the gold on them" is fine.
- Small children may ask how children can marry. You could add "who were grown up" after "your son and your daughter".

## Formatting
- Story 3 uses `## Lesson` headings, while stories 1 and 2 use `**Lesson:**`. Pick one style so the files stay consistent.
- Two titles have emoji and one does not.
- Optional extra frontmatter fields would make new stories easier to add to the app: `value` (e.g. Kindness), `age`, and a `tip` for parents.

Omitted ending: وَأَدْخِلْنِي بِرَحْمَتِكَ فِي عِبَادِكَ الصَّالِحِينَ, "And admit me by Your mercy into [the ranks of] Your righteous servants." **Dog story (fixed: source numbers, "wet mud", reward line): cite "Sahih al-Bukhari 2363 and Sahih Muslim 2244 (narrated by Abu Huraira)".** Bukhari 173, 2466 and 6009 tell the same story. It is not mixed up with the similar hadith about the woman who watered a dog (Bukhari 3467, Muslim 2245). Suggested text changes:
- "licking the dry earth" should be "licking the wet mud". The hadith word الثَّرَى means moist earth.
- Last line: "kindness that Allah loves" goes beyond the hadith. A closer version is "so we would remember that there is a reward for kindness to every living being" (فِي كُلِّ كَبِدٍ رَطْبَةٍ أَجْرٌ).
- Harmless additions that are not in the hadith: desert, hot sun, cool water, "Alhamdulillah, he felt better", "until it was happy".

**Ant story (fixed 2026-10-19):** "the speech of animals" is now "the speech of birds. He could even hear a tiny ant." (27:16). "So the great king stopped his whole army" is now "We can imagine the great king making sure his army stepped carefully around the little ants." The Quran does not say the army stopped.

## 4. Uwais and his mother (checked 2026-10-19)
Checked against Sahih Muslim 2542 on sunnah.com. The story is faithful: Uwais from Yemen, the skin illness healed except a spot the size of a coin (a dirham), his goodness to his mother, "ask him to pray for your forgiveness", Umar asking every group from Yemen, the three questions, Uwais making du'a for Umar, Kufa, the letter to the governor, and "I would rather be among the ordinary, poor people" (أَكُونُ فِي غَبْرَاءِ النَّاسِ). The Kufa part is in the narration numbered 2542c, so "Sahih Muslim 2542" covers the whole story.
- "If he asks Allah for something, Allah answers him" is a gentle paraphrase. The hadith says "if he were to swear by Allah, Allah would fulfil it" (لَوْ أَقْسَمَ عَلَى اللَّهِ لأَبَرَّهُ). Fine for children; no change made.
- "Allah loved him so much, because of how kindly he treated his mother" is a fair reading (the Prophet ﷺ names his goodness to his mother as what marks him out), but the hadith does not say it in those words.
- Pictures: the Prophet ﷺ is not shown. On the page where he speaks, Umar is shown listening in the mosque of Madinah.

## 5. Prophet Yunus and the big fish (checked 2026-10-20)
Checked against Surah Al-Anbiya 21:87-88, As-Saffat 37:139-148, Yunus 10:98 and Jami' at-Tirmidhi 3505. The story is faithful: Yunus leaving his people in anger, the laden ship (37:140), drawing lots (37:141), the fish swallowing him (37:142), the three darknesses (from the tafsir of "he called out within the darknesses", 21:87), the du'a, "So We answered him and saved him from distress; and thus do We save the believers" (21:88), being cast onto the shore while ill (37:145), the gourd plant (37:146), being sent to a hundred thousand or more (37:147), and their belief and enjoyment "for a time" (37:148, 10:98). The hadith in Tirmidhi 3505 says that no Muslim makes du'a with these words for anything without Allah answering him.
- "He would have stayed inside that fish for a very, very long time" softens 37:144, which says "until the Day they are resurrected". Fine for children; no change made.
- "Left the town before Allah told him to go" and the reason for drawing lots (the ship was too heavy) come from the classical tafsir, not the verses themselves. Both are the common explanation.
- Arabic of the du'a shown in the app is 21:87 (لَا إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا أَنْتَ سُبْحَانَكَ إِنِّي كُنْتُ مِنَ الظَّالِمِينَ), in modern spelling. Please have it checked like the ant story's dua.
- Pictures: Prophet Yunus is never shown. He appears only as a soft, warm light, including inside the fish.

## 6. The three men and the angel (checked 2026-10-21)
Checked against Sahih al-Bukhari 3464 and Sahih Muslim 2964 on sunnah.com. The story is faithful: three men of the Children of Israel (one with a skin disease, one bald, one blind), Allah testing them through an angel, each man's wish, the angel wiping over him, the pregnant she-camel, cow and sheep with "May Allah bless it for you", the valleys full of animals, the angel returning in each man's old form as a poor traveller, the first two denying ("the obligations are many", "I inherited it") and the warning "if you are lying, may Allah make you as you were", the third man's "take whatever you want", and "Allah is pleased with you and displeased with your two companions".
Changes made, with the user's go-ahead to correct this time:
- "Good skin and a nice color" is now "Good skin and a healthy color". The hadith's "good colour" is about the patches of the skin disease; "nice color" can sound to children like one skin colour is nicer than another. A judgement call.
- "he looked like a poor, tired traveler, looking just like each man used to look" is now "he looked like a poor, tired traveler, just like each man used to look" (repeated word).
- "The second man said the same thing" is now "The angel asked the second man the same way, and he said the same thing, so the angel said the same words to him", so it is clear the angel spoke first.
- Title in sentence case like the other books: "The three men and the angel".
Left as written:
- "The angel smiled" is not in the hadith. Harmless.
- In the hadith the angel also asks each man which animal he would like before giving it. The story leaves this out; fine for children.
- "I have too many things to pay for" is a fair child's wording of "the obligations are many" (الحقوق كثيرة), and "I got all this from my family" of "I inherited it from my forefathers".
- Pictures: the angel is never shown as a person. He is a cool silver light, and as the poor traveller a dim, dusty light with a walking staff and a travel bundle. The three men have no faces, as in every story.

## 7. The guest and the dark lamp (checked 2026-10-22)
Checked against Sahih al-Bukhari 3798 and Sahih Muslim 2054 on sunnah.com, and Surah Al-Hashr 59:9. The story is faithful: a hungry man comes to the Prophet ﷺ, his wives' homes have nothing but water, "Who will host this man tonight?", a man of the Ansar takes him home, his wife has only the children's food, they put the children to sleep, put out the lamp and pretend to eat, they go to bed hungry, and in the morning the Prophet ﷺ tells him what Allah thought of it, and 59:9 is revealed. Bukhari 4889 tells the same story; one narration in Muslim names the man as Abu Talha.
Changes made, with the user's go-ahead to correct:
- "a man from the Ansar, the kind people of Madinah" is now "a man from the Ansar, the helpers of Madinah". Ansar means "helpers", which is why they have that name.
- "Then, oops, the lamp went out!" is now "Then the wife got up as if to fix the lamp, and, oops, the lamp went out!" In the hadith she put it out on purpose while pretending to fix it; the old line could sound like an accident.
- "Allah mentioned it in the Quran forever" is now "Allah sent down a verse about it in the Quran that we still read today". The verse praises people who put others first; it does not name this family or retell the dinner.
- Title in sentence case like the other books: "The guest and the dark lamp".
Left as written:
- "Allah was pleased with what he and his wife had done" is a gentle wording. The hadith says Allah "laughed" or "marvelled" at it (ضَحِكَ اللَّهُ ... أَوْ عَجِبَ). Many children's books say "pleased"; a religious judgement call for the user.
- "Nobody had told the Prophet about it!" is not said in the hadith, but it is the clear sense: he knew from Allah.
- "He was very, very hungry" (Muslim: "I am exhausted"), "the guest ate until he was full" and "with happy hearts" are harmless additions.
- The Arabic shown under the verse page is the part of 59:9 that is quoted (وَيُؤْثِرُونَ عَلَىٰ أَنْفُسِهِمْ وَلَوْ كَانَ بِهِمْ خَصَاصَةٌ), in modern spelling. Please have it checked like the other Arabic.
- Pictures: the Prophet ﷺ is never shown, not even as a light. His pages show the guest, the companions in the palm-roofed mosque and his family's homes with only a jug of water. The guest, the husband, the wife and the children have no faces, as in every story.

## 8. Prophet Ayyub the patient (checked 2026-10-23)
Checked against Surah Al-Anbiya 21:83-84 and Surah Sad 38:41-44. The story is faithful: Ayyub's long illness and loss, his du'a "harm has touched me, and You are the Most Merciful of the merciful" (21:83), Allah answering him and removing his harm (21:84), "Strike the ground with your foot; this is a cool bath and a drink" (38:42), his family given back "and the like of them with them, as a mercy from Us and a reminder for those of understanding" (38:43, 21:84), and "We found him patient. What an excellent servant! He constantly turned back to Allah" (38:44). The story keeps to the Quran and leaves out the weak, gruesome details found in some older retellings, which is right for children.
Changes made:
- Title in sentence case like the other books: "Prophet Ayyub the patient". A judgement call: "the Patient" can also be read as a title, like a name, and could stay capitalised.
Left as written:
- "He lost many of the good things he once had" does not say that he lost his family, though a later page says Allah "gave him back his family". The classical tafsir says he lost his wealth and children. Gentle for children; no change made.
- "His body hurt, he grew tired and weak" and "He did not complain about Allah" come from the tafsir and the way the Quran praises his patience, not from the verses' words.
- In 38:44 Allah speaks as "We", so the verse says "he was one who constantly turns back", with "to Allah" understood. Adding "to Allah" is how many translations make it clear; fine.
- 38:41 (Ayyub saying Shaytan had touched him with hardship) and 38:44 (the bunch of grass and his oath) are left out. Fine for children.
- The Arabic shown on the du'a page is 21:83 (أَنِّي مَسَّنِيَ الضُّرُّ وَأَنْتَ أَرْحَمُ الرَّاحِمِينَ), in modern spelling. Please have it checked like the other Arabic.
- Pictures: at the user's request (2026-10-23), Prophet Ayyub is a figure made only of soft, warm light, with no face or features, and a shining star on his heart, so children can tell where he is. A single light was hard for children to follow. While he is ill the figure is paler, sitting on his mat, but it never goes out. His family have no faces, as in every story. Yunus is still shown only as a light.

## 9. Prophet Yusuf forgives his brothers (checked 2026-10-24)
Checked against Surah Yusuf. The story is faithful: the dream of eleven stars, the sun and the moon (12:4), Yaqub telling him not to share it and that Allah will choose him (12:5-6), the jealous brothers ("our father loves Yusuf more than us", 12:8), the well (12:10, 12:15), the evening return with tears and the wolf (12:16-17), "beautiful patience, and Allah is the One whose help is sought" (12:18), the travelers and the bucket (12:19-21), Yusuf put over the storehouses of Egypt (12:54-56), the brothers coming for food and not knowing him (12:58), "Do you know what you did with Yusuf?", "Are you Yusuf?", "I am Yusuf... Allah has been gracious to us" (12:89-90), "we have been sinners" (12:91), "No blame upon you today. Allah will forgive you, and He is the Most Merciful of the merciful" (12:92), and the family coming to Egypt, Yusuf taking his parents close, and the dream coming true (12:99-100).
Changes made:
- `source` was "12:4-18, 12:58-100". The travelers (12:19-21) and the king putting Yusuf over the food (12:54-56) are outside those verses, so it is now "12:4-21, 12:54-100 (especially 12:92)".
- "The dream came true, and the family was together again, all because Yusuf chose to forgive." is now "Allah made the dream come true, and the family was together again, because Yusuf chose to forgive." In 12:100 Yusuf himself says "my Lord has made it come true", so the dream came true by Allah's plan, not because of the forgiving. A judgement call; easy to change back.
- Title in sentence case like the other books: "Prophet Yusuf forgives his brothers".
Left as written:
- The story joins the brothers' three trips to Egypt into one and leaves out Binyamin (Benjamin), the cup in his bag and Yaqub's sight coming back with the shirt. In 12:89 Yusuf asks about "Yusuf and his brother". Fine for a short bedtime story.
- "Their father was very sad" is gentle: in 12:18 Yaqub also tells the brothers he does not believe them ("your souls have tempted you to something").
- The brothers' bad plan in 12:9-10 was first to kill him, and one brother talked them into the well instead. Left out, which is right for children.
- "May Allah forgive you" (يَغْفِرُ اللَّهُ لَكُمْ) is also translated "Allah will forgive you". Both are common; fine.
- 12:100 says the family bowed down to Yusuf, which is how the dream came true. The story only says the dream came true. Fine.
- The Arabic shown on the "No blame" page is 12:92 (لَا تَثْرِيبَ عَلَيْكُمُ الْيَوْمَ يَغْفِرُ اللَّهُ لَكُمْ وَهُوَ أَرْحَمُ الرَّاحِمِينَ), in modern spelling. Please have it checked like the other Arabic.
- Pictures: Prophet Yusuf and Prophet Yaqub are never pictured with faces or features. As with Ayyub, each is a figure made only of soft light with a star on the heart: Yusuf is small while he is a boy, and Yaqub is tall with a staff of light. The brothers, the travelers, the king and the family have no faces, as in every story. The dream is shown as eleven stars, the sun and the moon dipping down, as if bowing.
