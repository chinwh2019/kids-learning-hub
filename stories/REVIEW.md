# Story review

All three stories are warm, short and easy for children aged about 4 to 8 to follow. Each one names its source and ends with a lesson and a question, which is the right shape for bedtime. The app keeps your text word for word. The suggestions below are optional.

## 1. The man who helped a thirsty dog
- Faithful to the hadith (for example Sahih al-Bukhari 2363, Sahih Muslim 2244). Add the numbers to the `source` line, as story 3 does. Please check them against your own copy.
- The hadith says **"Allah thanked him and forgave him."** "Allah thanked him" is a beautiful detail for children and could replace "Allah was so pleased".
- The companions then asked whether there is reward for being kind to animals, and the Prophet ﷺ answered: "There is a reward for kindness to every living being." This fits your last paragraph and could be the closing line.

## 2. Prophet Sulayman and the little ant
- Surah An-Naml 27:16 says Sulayman was taught **the speech of birds**. "The speech of animals" goes a little further than the verse. You could say: "He taught him the speech of birds, and let him hear the tiny ant too."
- **"So the great king stopped his whole army"** is not stated in the Quran. It is a fair reading, but since the story cites the Quran, you could soften it: "We can imagine the great king made sure his army stepped carefully."
- The dua is shortened. The verse continues: "and admit me, by Your mercy, among Your righteous servants." Consider adding it. The app shows the Arabic of 27:19 (the first part) under that page, so please have it checked.
- Calling the ant "her" matches the Quran, which uses the feminine form. A fun fact for kids: the surah itself is named An-Naml, "The Ant".

## 3. The honest men and the jar of gold
- Faithful to Sahih al-Bukhari 3472 and Sahih Muslim 1721.
- In the hadith the judge says "spend on yourselves from it and give in charity", so "spend some of the gold on them" is fine.
- Small children may ask how children can marry. You could add "who were grown up" after "your son and your daughter".

## Formatting
- Story 3 uses `## Lesson` headings, while stories 1 and 2 use `**Lesson:**`. Pick one style so the files stay consistent.
- Two titles have emoji and one does not.
- Optional extra frontmatter fields would make new stories easier to add to the app: `value` (e.g. Kindness), `age`, and a `tip` for parents.
